# People associated with Plessey System 250

## Status

This is a working register of people currently known from source material or first-hand project recollection to have been associated with the Plessey System 250 (PP250), its software, architecture, development, applications, or contemporary technical documentation.

Entries distinguish direct evidence, published authorship, patent evidence and personal recollection. Inclusion does not by itself imply that a person was a member of the core System 250 development team. The patent-derived entries below should be read with `patents/PATENT-AUDIT.md`, which distinguishes original PP250/System 250 work from later architectural development.

## Plessey / System 250 people

### Anthony John Stanners

**Role:** CORAL 250 compiler project.

Anthony John Stanners worked on the CORAL 250 compiler from 1975 until 1977, finally serving as project leader during the maintenance phase.

**Evidence:** First-hand recollection of Anthony John Stanners.

### J. M. (Jack) Cotton

Associated with the architectural development of System 250. Henry M. Levy's later account states that Maurice Wilkes sent Cotton a draft of his book on time-sharing systems and that Cotton incorporated capability concepts into System 250.

Author of *The Operational Requirements for Future Communications Control Processors* (ISS, MIT, June 1972). Co-inventor on the foundational capability architecture, program-interrupt and capability-interrupt patent families.

**Evidence:** Published System 250 literature, patent records and Levy's historical account.

### D. M. England

Author of major contemporary papers describing System 250 architecture and operating-system design, including *Operating System of System 250* (1972), *Architectural Features of System 250* (1972), and *Capability Concept: Mechanisms and Structure in System 250* (1974).

**Evidence:** Published papers.

### David C. Cosserat

Author of *A Capability Oriented Multi-Processor System for Real-Time Applications* (ICCC, Washington, October 1972). Co-inventor on multiple original System 250 patent families covering capability registers, interrupts and multiprocessor structure.

**Evidence:** Published paper and patent records.

### D. Halton

Author of *Hardware of the System 250 for Communication Control* (ISS, MIT, June 1972).

**Evidence:** Published paper.

### Kenneth J. Hamer-Hodges

Author of *Fault Resistance and Recovery within System 250* (ICCC, Washington, October 1972). Co-inventor on original System 250 multiprocessor and fault-handling patents and on later capability/MCT allocation-deallocation work.

Hamer-Hodges later wrote *Civilizing Cyberspace: The Fight for Digital Democracy* (2020; later edition 2024). This has been identified as a potentially important later first-hand source: published material about PP250 cites the 2020 book, and Hamer-Hodges's public professional profile describes his capability-computer patents and later Church-Turing/security work. The project has **not yet examined the book itself**. A paper copy is available to the project.

**Research question:** Examine the book for explicit PP250/Plessey recollections, names of other participants, and Hamer-Hodges's own account of the relationship between System 250 and his later Church/capability/security thinking. Do not project the later interpretation backwards onto the 1970s machine without contemporary corroboration.

**Evidence:** Published System 250 paper; patent records; later published book/professional record.

### J. M. Crompton

Author of *Structure and Internal Communications of a Telephone Control System* (ICCC, Washington, October 1972).

**Evidence:** Published paper associated with the System 250 literature.

### W. A. C. Hemmings

Author of *Telephone Switching based on System 250* (ISS, MIT, June 1972).

**Evidence:** Published paper.

### D. J. Repton / Charles S. Repton

D. J. Repton is author of *Reliability Assurance for System 250: A Reliable, Real-Time Control System* (ICCC, Washington, October 1972). The patent corpus names Charles S. Repton as co-inventor with Peter C. Venton and Kenneth J. Hamer-Hodges on the PP250 fault-detection/handling family (US3814919A / GB1344474A).

**Status:** The relationship between the published initials D. J. Repton and patent inventor Charles S. Repton should not be assumed without identity corroboration.

**Evidence:** Published paper and patent records.

### Peter Bennett

Peter Bennett's public professional profile states that during industrial training at Plessey Telecom Research, Taplow Court, he worked on the design and construction of a distributed fault-tolerant multiprocessor computer system called the PP250.

**Evidence:** Public professional profile located during archival research, September 2026.

### Sir David Brown

Archives of IT documents Brown as having worked at Plessey Telecommunications Research, Taplow Court, on Ptarmigan, whose heart he describes as the PP250, and subsequently moving with the Ptarmigan switch to Plessey at Poole.

**Evidence:** Archives of IT oral-history interview.

## Other people directly relevant to the architectural history

### Maurice V. Wilkes

Cambridge computer scientist whose capability work influenced System 250. Levy records that Wilkes sent Jack Cotton a draft of his book containing a description of capabilities. Wilkes should not, on present evidence, be described as a Plessey System 250 engineer.

### Robert S. (Bob) Fabry

Worked on the University of Chicago Magic Number Machine. Levy records that Fabry later acted as a consultant for Plessey because of the relationship between the Chicago capability work and System 250.

## Current research and provenance contacts

These people are useful to the reconstruction project because of archival, documentary or professional-network connections. Inclusion here does not imply that they worked on System 250.

### Paul McJones

Software historian and collector associated with modern preservation of System 250 source material. McJones scanned surviving Plessey/System 250 documents discussed by the Retro Computing Forum in 2022. The provenance of those papers is a potential route to further internal documentation, particularly the missing System 250 Processor Instruction Set Manual.

### Alan Lindsay

Surfaced during investigation of Peter Bennett's professional network. At present no direct PP250/System 250 role has been established. Retain as a research lead only.

## Maintenance policy

For each additional person, record where possible:

- full name and initials;
- role or relationship to System 250;
- dates;
- project or subsystem;
- source/provenance;
- whether the information is primary evidence, secondary evidence or personal recollection;
- unresolved questions about identity or role.

Do not convert recollection into documentary fact without corroboration, and do not infer that authors or patent inventors necessarily held a particular organisational role unless the evidence establishes it.
