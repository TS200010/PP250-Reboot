# People associated with Plessey System 250

## Status

This is a working register of people currently known from source material or first-hand project recollection to have been associated with the Plessey System 250 (PP250), its software, architecture, development, applications, or contemporary technical documentation.

Entries should distinguish direct evidence, published authorship, and personal recollection. Inclusion does not by itself imply that a person was a member of the core System 250 development team.

## Plessey / System 250 people

### Anthony John Stanners

**Role:** CORAL 250 compiler project.

Anthony John Stanners worked on the CORAL 250 compiler from 1975 until 1977, finally serving as project leader during the maintenance phase.

**Evidence:** First-hand recollection of Anthony John Stanners.

### J. M. (Jack) Cotton

Associated with the architectural development of System 250. Henry M. Levy's later account states that Maurice Wilkes sent Cotton a draft of his book on time-sharing systems and that Cotton incorporated capability concepts into System 250.

Cotton was author of:

- *The Operational Requirements for Future Communications Control Processors*, International Switching Symposium, MIT, June 1972.

**Evidence:** Published System 250 literature and Levy's historical account.

### D. M. England

Author of major contemporary papers describing System 250 architecture and operating-system design, including:

- *Operating System of System 250*, International Switching Symposium, MIT, June 1972.
- *Architectural Features of System 250*, 1972.
- *Capability Concept: Mechanisms and Structure in System 250*, 1974.

**Evidence:** Published papers.

### Nigel J. Wheatley

Nigel J. Wheatley is documented as co-inventor with Martyn P. Andrews on multiple Plessey patents directly relevant to System 250 architecture:

- US 4,408,274 — *Memory protection system using capability registers*.
- US 4,486,831 — *Multi-programming data processing system process suspension*.
- US 4,383,297 — *Data processing system including internal register addressing arrangements*.

The patents provide documentary evidence of Wheatley's involvement with System 250 capability, process-state and internal-register mechanisms.

**Evidence:** US patent records.

### Peter Bennett

Peter Bennett's public professional profile states that during industrial training at **Plessey Telecom Research, Taplow Court, Buckinghamshire**, he worked on the design and construction of a distributed fault-tolerant multiprocessor computer system called the **PP250**.

This is unusually direct modern evidence of personal involvement with the PP250 hardware/system development and makes Bennett a potentially valuable source of first-hand recollection and leads to surviving technical documentation or other former engineers.

**Evidence:** Public professional/LinkedIn profile located during archival research, September 2026.

**Research value:** Potential first-hand source for PP250 development and for the provenance/survival of internal processor and instruction-set documentation.

### Sir David Brown

Sir David Brown is documented in an Archives of IT oral-history interview as having worked at **Plessey Telecommunications Research, Taplow Court**, and subsequently in work involving the PP250/Ptarmigan environment. The interview provides an independent route into the surviving Plessey engineering community.

**Evidence:** Archives of IT oral-history interview.

**Research value:** Potential first-hand source and possible route to other former PP250 engineers or personal papers.

## Other people directly relevant to the architectural history

### Maurice V. Wilkes

Cambridge computer scientist whose work on capability systems influenced System 250. Levy records that Wilkes sent Jack Cotton a draft of his book containing a description of capabilities.

Wilkes should not, on present evidence, be described as a Plessey System 250 engineer.

**Evidence:** Levy's historical account.

### Robert S. (Bob) Fabry

Worked on the University of Chicago Magic Number Machine. Levy records that Fabry later acted as a consultant for Plessey because of the relationship between the Chicago capability work and System 250.

**Evidence:** Levy's historical account.

## People known from first-hand project recollection but requiring documentary corroboration

### Martyn Phillip Andrews

Martyn Phillip Andrews is documented as a Plessey inventor on multiple patents directly relevant to System 250 architecture. He is also recalled by Anthony John Stanners as a project manager associated with the CORAL 250 work.

Relevant US patents include:

- US 4,408,274 — *Memory protection system using capability registers* — Martyn P. Andrews and Nigel J. Wheatley; assigned to Plessey Overseas Limited.
- US 4,486,831 — *Multi-programming data processing system process suspension* — Martyn P. Andrews and Nigel J. Wheatley; assigned to Plessey Overseas Limited.
- US 4,383,297 — *Data processing system including internal register addressing arrangements* — Martyn P. Andrews and Nigel J. Wheatley; assigned to Plessey Overseas Limited.

These patents provide documentary evidence connecting Andrews directly with System 250 capability, process-state and internal-register mechanisms.

**Evidence:** US patent records; first-hand recollection of Anthony John Stanners.

**Recollection:** Stanners recalls Andrews as a project manager associated with the CORAL 250 work.

### Ian Cottam

Recalled by Anthony John Stanners as a colleague on the System 250/CORAL 250 work; Stanners recalls that Cottam worked at the desk opposite him.

**Evidence:** First-hand recollection of Anthony John Stanners.

**Status:** Further documentary evidence sought.

### John Purdy / Purdey

A person recalled in connection with the PP250/CORAL project. The spelling of the surname and precise role remain unresolved.

**Evidence:** First-hand project recollection.

**Status:** Identity and role require corroboration.

## Current research and provenance contacts

The people in this section are useful to the reconstruction project because of archival, documentary, or professional-network connections. Inclusion here does **not** imply that they worked on System 250.

### Paul McJones

Paul McJones is a software historian and collector associated with the modern preservation of System 250 source material. During the 2022 Retro Computing Forum discussion of System 250, McJones scanned surviving Plessey/System 250 documents that were subsequently made publicly available online, including important contemporary architectural material.

The provenance of the physical papers he scanned is therefore a potentially important route to further internal Plessey documentation, particularly the missing **System 250 Processor Instruction Set Manual**.

**Evidence:** Modern archival provenance trail associated with the 2022 scans and uploads.

**Research question:** Where did the original documents come from, what other System 250 material accompanied them, and does the source collection contain processor/instruction-set documentation that has not yet been digitised?

### Alan Lindsay

Alan Lindsay surfaced during investigation of Peter Bennett's professional network. Bennett's public professional profile contains a recommendation associated with Lindsay, described in that context as a systems architect.

At present **no direct PP250 or System 250 role has been established for Lindsay**. He is retained as a research lead because the connection to Bennett may place him within, or close to, the relevant Plessey engineering network.

**Evidence:** Professional-network connection discovered through Peter Bennett's public profile.

**Status:** Unverified PP250 connection. Do not describe as a System 250 engineer without further evidence.

**Research question:** Establish identity, Plessey employment history, dates, engineering role, and whether there was any PP250/System 250 involvement.

## Maintenance policy

For each additional person, record where possible:

- full name and initials;
- role or relationship to System 250;
- dates;
- project or subsystem;
- source/provenance;
- whether the information is primary evidence, secondary evidence, or personal recollection;
- unresolved questions about identity or role.

Do not convert recollection into documentary fact without corroboration, and do not infer that authors of papers were necessarily members of the core development team unless the evidence establishes that relationship.
