## Direct drawing evidence — PRIMARY EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION

### Organisation, symbols and labels

The top part carries grouped data and code lines, gated paths and rectangular comparison/encoding blocks. The lower part carries control gates and the HSC state counter. Dash-dot boundaries group the circuitry; long signal lines cross numerous other lines. A crossing alone is not treated as a connection: bends, branch points and arrowheads must support the trace.

Rectangular named blocks are:
- CODE COMP, on 5a;
- DATA COMP, D & STROBE GEN, SENSE and CODER, on 5b;
- HSC, with individual state positions along the bottom of 5c–5d.

The word/bus gates GD1, GD2, GD3 and GD4 are circular symbols marked “&”. GD5 is drawn as a numbered circle, containing 2. Control gates have GC references and circles containing 1, 2, 3, 5 or 6. These printed numbers are retained below; they are not reference numerals for separate physical components.

Named inverter symbols I1, I2, I3, I4 and I5 appear in the control paths. The small square/triangle shapes and overbars must not be discarded when reading their associated conditions.

The drawing uses:
- state numerals 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, also in complemented form at some gate inputs;
- buffer-state labels B0 and B2, distinct from the HSC numerals;
- code-condition labels in parentheses, for example (A), (B), (H) and (M);
- unparenthesised names including H, F, R, TT, NIR, DBUS, TXSS, TXSF, RXSS, SS and DS;
- comparator labels C≡ and D≡. The three-horizontal-stroke mark is transcribed as ≡; it is not replaced by a guessed word or Boolean equality.

### External data, code and strobe interfaces

At the right of 5b, the upper grouped routes are labelled DATA SENSE and DATA LAUNCH, with endpoint wire designations D1 and D10. DATA SENSE is received toward the comparator/selection circuitry; DATA LAUNCH has outward right-facing arrows. Intermediate members of the ten-wire group are represented by the bus convention rather than ten separately labelled wires.

Below them:
- INHIBIT DATA THROUGH (IDT) leaves to the right from GC7.
- CODE SENSE is labelled C1 and C4 at its endpoint lines. Its routes enter SENSE and continue left across the 5b/5a join.
- CODE LAUNCH is likewise labelled C1 and C4, with outward right-facing arrows. It receives the local code-register gated route and CODER's output.
- STROBE LAUNCH leaves toward the right from the D & STROBE GEN output route.
- STROBE SENSE enters the control circuitry lower down; the abbreviation SS is distributed on the continued control paths.
- The lower right-hand control area includes INHIBIT STROBE THROUGH (IST) and INHIBIT CODE THROUGH (ICT); ICT is driven from GC10.

The labels DD and CD identify the adjoining drive portions. This figure does not show the internal diode/transistor construction of those portions.

At the left-hand joins toward Figure 4 are the transmission-selection/feedback lines TXSS and TXSF, hardware/address/data connections, H1/H4/H6 references, B0, B2, and the comparison/strobe/control returns DR≡, CR≡, SST and SSR/SRR. Further lower routes include NIR, TT, R, F and DBUS. RXSS and CR(F) also occur in the continued control wiring. The exact SSR/SRR letter order is difficult at some reduced-size labels; the separately discussed prose supplies context without changing the visible uncertainty.

### Data routes across 5a–5b

GD1 is an “&” gate on a left-to-right data route in 5a. Its input comes from the buffer-side grouped data/address path, and its output continues across the join into the DATA LAUNCH group. Its control is associated with the GC4/state-1 selection network.

GD2 is an “&” gate higher on 5a. The arrow into it is from the right; its grouped output goes left toward the device-input/buffer interface. Thus this is a right-to-left receiving route, distinct from GD1's launch route. Its control comes through the state-6/GC35 selection distribution.

GD3 and GD4 are “&” gates in the local comparator-operand selection routes. Their outputs join the grouped operand path that continues right to DATA COMP. The control branch contains an inverter, so the two depicted selections must not be read as permanently connected parallel sources. The crowded left-hand bus origins cannot all be resolved into individual pins from the figure alone; their priority-address/hardware-address functions are identified by the prose below.

DATA COMP receives the sensed-data group and the selected local comparison operand. Only endpoint lines of the grouped operands are drawn. Its outputs are:
- D≡, directed left into the qualification network, including the numbered GD5 gate;
- LNOH, descending into the lower control region.

GD5 is a circle marked 2. It receives the D≡ comparison route and another control input; its output returns left into the data-equivalence qualification route. It is not another rectangular comparator, nor is it an “&” word gate merely because its reference begins GD.

The selection branches and the DATA LAUNCH/DATA SENSE/comparison routes share the same upper horizontal band, but the drawing does not imply that every crossing there is electrically joined.

### Code comparison, reception and launch

CODE COMP on 5a has grouped code connections from the CODE SENSE side and the local buffer/code-register side. The illustrated endpoint lines stand for the group. Its C≡ output leaves toward the left into control/comparison qualification.

GC5 is an “&” gate with right-to-left arrows on the received-code route. It admits the sensed-code group toward the Figure 4 decoder interface. Its control comes from GC4.

GC22 is an “&” gate with left-to-right arrows on the local code-register route. It passes the grouped code toward CODE LAUNCH. Its control comes from GC20.

GC4 is a circle marked 1. It receives the state-1 selection and the GC35-related selection branch, then distributes control to the data/code paths. GC35 is marked 2 and includes a state-6 input; it also participates in the receive-side data selection.

The nearby numbered control network includes:
- GC19, marked 2, with a state-1 input and a common qualification route;
- GC21, marked 2, receiving comparison/qualification wiring associated with C≡;
- GC20, marked 1, combining the GC21-side result with another control route and controlling GC22;
- GC47, marked 2, with state 1 and the condition (H);
- GC14, marked 2, with state 1 and D≡, returning DR≡ toward the buffer;
- CR≡ returned toward the buffer from the code-comparison qualification network.

C≡/D≡, the qualified CR≡/DR≡ returns and individual launch/sense buses are different labelled paths. The long shared-looking lines should not be collapsed into one generic “compare” signal.

SENSE on 5b receives the C1/C4 endpoint routes from CODE SENSE. Downward outputs are explicitly labelled (A) and (M). This block is separate from CODE COMP; it is also separate from the decoder reached through GC5.

CODER is drawn below SENSE. Its output rises to the C1/C4 CODE LAUNCH group. On its left:
- LB is supplied from GC8;
- the middle L-prefixed label reads LK in the inspected scan, attached to the direct state-5 route;
- LJ is supplied from GC33.
LG enters from below. The middle printed label is retained as an uncertain LK/LL reading because its form and the prose's busy-response description do not agree cleanly.

GC8 is marked 2 and has state 1 plus the B2-related route through inverter I4. Its output goes right to LB. GC33 is marked 2, with state 6 and B0, and goes right to LJ. The state-5 line enters the middle CODER input directly. LG is connected to the lower fault-related route.

### Data and strobe generator

D & STROBE GEN is a rectangle on the upper part of 5b. Along its lower side, upward arrows are labelled P1, P2, P3, P4, P5 and P6. The drawing depicts distinct pulse-request inputs; it does not print pulse lengths or delay values.

DS leaves the generator toward the left and is distributed into control qualification. Another output route goes right and then down to STROBE LAUNCH.

The associated request/control paths include:
- GC24, marked 1, combining state 2 and state 5;

- GC16, marked 1, combining the GC15 output and NIR;

The request lines continue vertically into the P-input region. Individual pulse assignments explained in the patent prose are kept in the prose section; proximity of a vertical wire to another input is not used to invent a connection.

### Data/code/strobe-through inhibition

GC6, circle 2, receives the common data-control route and a complemented state-2 condition. Its output goes right to GC7, circle 3. GC7 also receives complemented state 0 and another qualification line. Its rightward output is INHIBIT DATA THROUGH (IDT).

In the lower-right area GC9, circle 1, receives state inputs 1, 2, 5 and 6. Its output enters GC10, circle 3. GC10 also has a complemented-state-0 input and a control qualification; its output goes right to INHIBIT CODE THROUGH (ICT).

In 5c, GC11 is visibly a circle containing 6. Five inputs are individually labelled 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6; a further input comes from the GC27 route. Its output continues into GC12. GC12 is marked 3 and also receives complemented state 0 and the I5-associated condition. Its output continues across the join toward the IST route in 5d.

The number 6 inside GC11 is not silently changed to 1. Nor is a complete Boolean equation inferred by treating all five mutually exclusive state labels as simultaneously true. The drawing's literal marking and the explanatory description's general symbol convention are both recorded, with the conflict left visible.

The receive/strobe return network on 5a also includes GC34, marked 2, with state 6 and the DS-related qualification, returning a signal toward the buffer; and GC36, marked 2, in the SS/state-qualified return route labelled SSR. ### HSC state counter

The bottom rectangle is labelled HSC. It spans the 5c/5d join:
- S0, S1 and S2 are on 5c;
- S3, S4, S5 and S6 are on 5d.

There are seven visibly drawn positions. The corresponding outputs 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 leave downward, then feed the long distribution routes rising into the control gates. Gate input numerals elsewhere refer back to these state lines; B0 and B2 belong to separate buffer-state routes.

Control-entry paths enter the counter from above. ## Explicit explanation supplied by the patent prose — SECONDARY EVIDENCE

These statements come from the patent text/OCR, not solely from graphical shape or spatial placement.

### Unit and symbol meanings

The text identifies Figure 5 as the highway drive control unit and Figure 4 as the associated buffer unit. It describes a highway having ten data wires, four code wires and one strobe wire. It explains circles marked 1 as OR gates, circles marked greater than 1 as AND gates, and “&” symbols as gates for groups of wires.

The text describes HSC as selecting one state at a time. Its OCR wording includes “seven states” followed by a range that reads S0 to S7. The drawing actually shows S0–S6. This capture follows the seven positions visible in the drawing, without inventing S7.

The prose's displayed operating expressions are described as sequence traces rather than Boolean equations. They are therefore used to explain operation, not as authority to redraw individual ambiguous junctions.

### Sending and arbitration

The text associates local priority address and hardware address selection with the GD gate network and DATA COMP. During a sending attempt, TXSS and the GC1/GC2 selection path put HSC into S1. Failure to obtain selection returns TXSF through GC3.

In state 1, the prose traces selection through GC4/GC5, interruption of data through GC6/GC7, priority-address launch through GD1, code-B generation through GC8/LB and the code-through inhibition through GC9/GC10. It also describes strobe interruption through GC11/GC12. These functional descriptions do not resolve the unusual printed 6 inside GC11.

If LNOH reports the arbitration condition described in the text, GC13 changes the control to S3 for receiving. When the local priority returns equal, state 1 and the data-comparison qualification use GC14 to return the data-equality indication toward the buffer.

NIR can request a strobe through GC15/GC16 and P5. State 1 plus sensed strobe uses GC17/SST toward the buffer's strobe circuitry. State 1 plus repeat request R uses GC18 and P4. Timeout TT in state 1 uses GC23 to select S2; GC24 combines the state-2/state-5 conditions for the described P2 response.

### Receiving, busy response and release

The prose's (B)-with-state-0 route through GC26 corresponds to the drawing's GR26 and enters S3. A sensed B code with equal data in S3 qualifies GC27 and then GC28 with SS. Availability/buffer-state conditions through GC29 select S4; the alternate GCC route selects S5 for a busy response.

In S4 the device-side receive selection is performed, then RXSS through GC32 selects S6. In S6, B0 through GC33 requests the J response from CODER. GC35 and GC4 enable the receive data/code paths. The delayed strobe/control route through GC34 returns SRR toward the buffer, while GC36 provides the SSR-related return.

The text describes the state-5 coder response as the busy code. This is why the middle coder input's printed LK/LL reading has been flagged rather than silently rewritten. The visible state-5 connection is firmer evidence than the uncertain second letter.

The release code A, outside the excluded state conditions, uses GC38 and GC39 with SS to return HSC to S0. The state-2 release path is separately associated with GC31.

### Response changeover and faults

The text associates CR(F) in state 1 through GC40, followed by the delayed-strobe qualification at GC41, with entry into S6. The received F/changeover sequence uses GC42, GC43 and GC44 to request P6 and an H response. H in state 6 through GC46 participates in the complementary return sequence. These are prose explanations of the visible feedback/request circuitry, not additional drawn hardware blocks.

A buffer fault condition in state 1 uses the gate printed GC1F and the generator/coder paths associated with LG and P3. R can request repetition through P4. The prose also discusses the H-related comparison gate GC47. The named F fault route and the received code condition involving F must not be conflated merely because both contain the same letter.

## Uncertainties and transcription limits

- The circuit is connected across all four sheets. Remaining uncertainty concerns particular crowded control nets and lettering, not whether these are four separate circuits.
- GC11 visibly contains 6, although the general prose symbol convention would make its multiple one-state inputs problematic if converted mechanically into a simultaneous AND expression. No correction or replacement netlist is asserted.
- GR26 is the drawing reading; GC26 is the prose reference. GCC is retained literally.
- The CODER's middle input appears LK, with LL a plausible reading in context; state 5's connection is visible. Its spelling is not resolved by inventing a different code.
- SSR/SRR, GC1F/GCIF, and some tiny overbars/parentheses are vulnerable to the scan's lettering. The distinct labelled routes are recorded without treating uncertain glyphs as reliable inversions or code identities.
- Named comparators and the D & STROBE GEN are functional boxes. The drawing does not disclose their internal gates, comparator bit ordering, pulse widths or delay values here.
- Only endpoint members are explicitly labelled for the grouped data/code connections. The capture does not invent individual D2–D9/C2–C3 pin wiring.
- The state-counter entry network is densely crossed. Confirmed gate/condition associations are preserved, while unlabelled crossings or closely adjacent wires are not promoted to proven junctions.

INTERPRETIVE NOTE

**Figure evidence:** the drawing separates sensed-versus-launched data/code, explicit through-inhibition controls, a six-input pulse-request generator interface, comparison paths and a shared seven-position state counter. It visibly crosses all four sheet boundaries.

**Patent-text evidence:** the prose identifies a highway drive controller which arbitrates sending, selects receiving, handles busy and changeover responses, and controls strobe timing in conjunction with the separate buffer unit.

**Architectural inference:** the combination supports reading Figure 5 as a coordinated control/data-path circuit rather than four independent subsystems. Its behaviour depends on local buffer state, highway code/data comparisons and sensed strobes. This does not establish an internal microprogram, processor instruction set, hidden comparator implementation or a complete Boolean netlist beyond what the drawing and prose explicitly disclose.
