# FIGURES 2a AND 2b — TEXTUAL CAPTURE

Patent PDF: `patents/US-3771146-A.pdf` — US 3,771,146, patented November 6, 1973.
Figure/page identifiers: `Fig. 2a`, `SHEET 02 OF 11`, PDF page 3 of 22; `Fig. 2b`, `SHEET 03 OF 11`, PDF page 4 of 22 (one-based).
Transcription consulted: `transcriptions/US3771146A.rtf`, “BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS”; “GENERAL DESCRIPTION”, particularly “PROCESSOR MODULE DESCRIPTION”, paragraphs (10)–(35), the indicator/zero-detector/trap-detector descriptions, and the later memory-access, trap and load-capability accounts.

These are two halves of **one continuous processor diagram**. The patent explicitly instructs that they be placed side by side with Figure 2b on the right. They are captured together here; right-edge continuations of 2a are followed onto 2b rather than treated as independent circuits.

The actual PDF pages were inspected at 280 dpi, including enlarged upper/lower crops of both halves. The downloaded PDF’s Git blob identity was checked against the repository: `faa0cd1fa897835d9f6d9be2e9bbeca9b102272b`. The PDF remains the primary source. Gate identities, visible directions and prose-supplied explanations are distinguished below.

## Direct drawing evidence — PRIMARY EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION

### Combined arrangement and symbols

On the left half, the upper register row contains ACC STACK with SELA, BASE STACK with SELB, and RES REG. MILL is below them. PIR and SIR are below/right of MILL. On the right half, IR is at the upper right; OP REG and the combined TC/LMT STK with SELL lie below the control unit; ZD, TD, COMP, FIR and SDIREG occupy the lower region.

A single large microprogram-control rectangle straddles the join. Its left edge and some inputs/outputs appear on 2a; its name µPROG and right edge appear on 2b. It is not two control units. AUCS, CIS and TI enter its top on 2a; ICS, FICS, OP=0 and instruction-derived inputs enter on 2b.

The main highway MHW is drawn with a heavy line: it runs across the upper data-register region, descends on the right of the main datapath, and returns across the bottom of both halves. MILL’s result joins its bottom stretch. It is a shared distribution/feedback highway, not a CPU boundary.

Circles containing 2 are gating symbols with additional short control-input arrows. Circles containing 1 combine inputs. Gate names G1–G20 identify particular numbered gates; the internal numerals 1 and 2 are symbol codes, not those gate identities. Register-wide inputs/outputs are frequently depicted as two arrowed strokes collected by a brace. These braces must not be mistaken for two independent registers or an asserted two-bit width.

### Main-highway register paths and arithmetic inputs

**Accumulator path:** MHW branches downward through G10 into the braced upper inputs of ACC STACK. RSEL reaches SELA with leftward arrows into its selection compartment. ACC STACK’s braced lower output passes through G7 and enters the left side of an OR symbol containing 1. That OR symbol feeds one upper input of MILL.

**Base paths:** BASE STACK’s upper input comes through G20 from the SOH-derived thin route. Its lower output branches to two separately controlled gates:

- G5 feeds the combined first MILL-input network and the right-going comparison route.
- G18 feeds a separate right-going arithmetic-operand route, joining the OR network on 2b.

CRSEL reaches SELB and continues toward SELL. BASE STACK and TC/LMT STK are therefore explicitly coordinated across the split by selection wiring, not merely similar names in separate diagrams.

**Result path:** MHW branches through G15 into RES REG. RES REG’s output passes through G16, then runs left into the right side of the OR symbol feeding the first MILL input.

**Operand path:** On 2b, MHW branches through G6, while the SOH/G2 route branches through G3. G6 and G3 converge at an OR symbol containing 1, whose output feeds OP REG. OP REG’s lower output branches to ZD and downward through G4.

G4 enters a second OR symbol containing 1. That symbol also receives the G18 continuation from BASE STACK, a TC/LMT-derived route through G12, and the GOS route from µPROG. Its output crosses left to the second upper input of MILL.

These OR nodes combine alternative paths; the drawing does not show all sources being enabled simultaneously. Gate control stubs are separate from the data inputs.

**MILL:** Two upper data inputs arrive through the two combining networks. AUµS arrows enter its left side. Rightward condition-output strokes are bracketed AUCS and lead to an upward output arrow. Additional MILL outputs run to PIR. Its braced lower result output joins the heavy bottom MHW, closing the common register-distribution route.

### Memory-return and instruction paths

On the upper right, SOH is shown entering from the right by leftward arrows collected by a brace. It branches:

- Upward into G1, whose output feeds the braced right-side inputs of IR.
- Leftward into G2, whose output distributes over the thin route to G3, G19 and the cross-boundary G20 route.

IR’s braced left-side outputs run toward the control unit’s upper instruction-related inputs. SOHCS enters from the right and is routed to the corresponding right-side µPROG inputs; a branch from the control-signal region also runs down toward COMP.

IR, OP REG, BASE STACK and TC/LMT STK are distinct destinations; G1 and G2 must not be treated as one gate or one destination.

### TC/LMT output, trap detection and access-code paths

G19 feeds the upper braced inputs of TC/LMT STK from the SOH-derived route. SELL receives the CRSEL continuation from 2a.

The braced lower output divides into several paths:

- A rightward branch enters TD.
- An AC route passes through G13 and returns upward toward the right-side AC inputs of µPROG.
- Another branch passes through G12 into the lower operand-combining OR gate, whose output goes left to MILL.
- A downward route reaches COMP’s upper input wiring.

TD outputs downward, then left along TI across the join to the SIR input region. A branch also turns upward to an arrow labelled TI on 2b. µPROG has a top input with that same name on 2a. TD itself is only a rectangle here; its internal test is not drawn.

### Zero detection

OP REG’s output branches right into ZD independently of its gated G4 arithmetic path. ZD has an upward output labelled OP=0. µPROG’s top has a matching OP=0 input on 2b. No internal ZD gates, bit groups or comparator symbols are drawn in Figure 2.

### Comparator, address/output register and memory request paths

COMP receives:

- Upper inputs from the base-comparison continuation and TC/LMT output route.
- A lower input from SDIREG’s output wiring.
- Right-side inputs from the returning control-signal route and the separate short input labelled CµS.

A rightward COMP output is labelled CIS. Additional leftward output strokes feed FIR’s indicator-input region.

MHW’s right-side vertical stretch branches through G11 into SDIREG’s braced left inputs. +1S enters SDIREG from above. Its braced right output branches toward COMP’s lower input and through G14 to an external rightward arrow labelled SIH.

µPROG’s lower right-side output route descends to an external rightward arrow labelled SIHCS. No external memory module is drawn in these two halves.

### Indicator paths

**PIR:** Receives condition-related paths from MILL, top controls labelled µPIC, and a short unlabelled right-side input. Its braced lower output branches to ICS and passes through G17 to bottom MHW.

**SIR:** Receives TI from the cross-boundary TD route. Top control-input groups are labelled µSIC on both sides of that TI entry. A short unlabelled input arrow enters its right side. Its braced lower output branches to ICS and passes through a circle containing 2 to bottom MHW. No separate G-number is clearly printed beside that SIR readout gate.

**FIR:** Receives COMP condition paths, controls labelled µFIC and a G8-gated route arriving from the left continuation. Its lower output branches to FICS and passes through G9 to bottom MHW.

ICS and FICS also appear as top inputs to µPROG. The drawing does not expand the individual indicator bits in PIR, SIR or FIR.

## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

The brief description explicitly specifies the side-by-side join with 2b on the right. The processor description explains:

- ACC STK, BASE STK and TC/LMT STK can be scratch-pad units with line selectors SELA, SELB and SELL.
- BASE STK and TC/LMT STK contain corresponding halves of each capability register: base information in one, limit and permitted access code in the other.
- MHW, SIH and SOH are typically 24-bit parallel highways. The drawings simplify these to single lines. SOH/SIH connect to memory through the intercommunication medium; the memory itself is omitted.
- Circles containing 2 denote AND gating, with unreferenced control inputs driven by µPROG; a highway gate represents multiple physical gates. Circles containing 1 denote OR isolation/combination. This supplies the logical meaning of the symbols, rather than their merely visible numerals.
- RES REG temporarily holds results from MHW. OPREG can receive MHW or SOH information and is used in address formation. IR holds instruction control fields.
- µPROG controls gate operations, MILL over AUµS, COMP over CµS, indicators over µPIC/µSIC/µFIC, selectors over RSEL/CRSEL, SDIREG incrementing over +1S, and memory access over SIHCS.
- MILL’s condition outputs are AUCS; COMP’s are CIS; indicator returns are ICS and FICS; ZD supplies OP=0.
- Despite its name “memory-data-input register”, SDIREG is explicitly described as the processor-to-memory output register: address/data is assembled there before transmission over SIH.
- TD monitors the two most significant access-code bits and supplies TI to the secondary trap indicator. ZD tests whether OPREG’s contents are all zero. Their internal circuits are given in other figures, not inside the Figure 2 blocks.
- The trap account explicitly says G8 loads FIR’s four most significant bits with the current CRSEL state.
- The later load-capability account describes GOS as carrying a microprogram-generated offset used with a selected base in MILL.

These statements identify purposes and widths not individually printed on every drawing line. They do not add internal gates to ZD, TD, COMP or µPROG in this capture.

## Uncertainties, discrepancies and limits

- The short labels OP=0 and TI use glyphs resembling O/0 and I/1 in this scan. They are normalised according to the matching prose and repeated signal labels, not interpreted as new component numbers.
- A few light strokes at register-output braces and gate intersections are degraded. Their surrounding continuous routes and arrows are recorded; an intersection without a clear branch has not been treated as an extra connection.
- The SIR readout gate has a visible 2 but no confidently legible G-number. It is not assigned an invented missing gate identity.
- PIR and SIR have short unlabelled side-input arrows whose exact source is not established by those stubs alone.
- The two occurrences of µSIC around SIR are preserved as drawn; separate signal names or functions are not invented.
- G19/G20 are visibly fed from the thin upper route associated with SOH/G2. The later prose also describes loading zero from MILL/MHW into both capability halves by activating G19/G20. The precise additional steering needed for that operation is not unambiguously shown by this overview; no new MHW connection is silently inserted to reconcile it.
- Control stubs do not individually name every µPECS signal. No timing, microinstruction encoding, enable arbitration or simultaneous-driving behaviour is inferred from the overview.
- Numbers of drawn braced strokes are not register widths; the 24-bit highway description is supplied by prose.
- The comparison, trap and capability-loading sequences span additional figures and prose. Figure 2 alone does not show every state transition or indicator bit.
- These are limits of this source capture, not declarations that the corresponding architectural mechanisms remain unresolved elsewhere in the repository.

INTERPRETIVE NOTE

**Explicit figure evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** The joined drawing connects capability selection and separate base/type-limit paths to arithmetic, bounds/access comparison, access-code monitoring and indicator control. TD’s TI route returns across the join to SIR; the datapath and MHW also continue across both halves.

**Explicit patent-text evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** The halves form one processor; the paired stacks implement capability registers, COMP checks bounds/access, and TD signals the trap condition. µPROG controls their sequence.

**Architectural inference:** Reading the halves as one diagram preserves the relation between capability use, trap indication and microprogram control that would be lost if they were captured independently. The overview does not justify adding unshown control states, hand-offs or circuitry.
