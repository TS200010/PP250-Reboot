## Direct drawing evidence — PRIMARY EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION

### Register unit and visible inventory

The drawing is labelled RU, REGISTER UNIT, near its centre. It shows two columns of horizontal register rectangles arranged in five rows:

| Row | Left register and printed description | Right register and printed description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | AAR — “A” ADDRESS REGISTER | BAR — “B” ADDRESS REGISTER |
| Second | SCR — SEQUENCE CONTROL REGISTER | LIR — LINK REGISTER |
| Third | CBR — CHARACTER BASE REGISTER | HDR — HIGHWAY DATA REGISTER |
| Fourth | MR — “M” COUNT REGISTER | NR — “N” COUNT REGISTER |
| Fifth | CPA — “A” CHARACTER POSITION REGISTER | CPB — “B” CHARACTER POSITION REGISTER |

CPA and CPB have much smaller rectangles than the other eight registers. There is no surrounding RU box. The long perimeter-like strokes are connected highway paths, not merely an enclosure.

All ten registers have circular gate symbols on incoming and outgoing routes. The circles are empty, with no individual gate numbers. Micro-control leads into those circles are not separately drawn.

### Common data-path structure

Two long incoming routes arrive from the lower right, rise near the right side and branch left toward the register inputs. The endpoint markings are 1 and 10. Two outgoing routes gather register outputs, run down the left side and turn right along the bottom, again with endpoint markings 1 and 10.

The paired endpoint strokes depict a grouped highway. They are not labelled as two separate independently addressed buses. Each of AAR, BAR, SCR, LIR, CBR, HDR, MR and NR has:

- Two illustrated input-endpoint routes, each passing through an empty circle and entering the register with an arrow.
- Two illustrated output-endpoint routes, each passing from the register downward through an empty circle to the collecting routes.
- Incoming branches from the common incoming highway routes.
- Outgoing connections into the common outgoing highway routes.

AAR and BAR inputs descend from the top horizontal branches. SCR/LIR, CBR/HDR and MR/NR inputs branch left at their respective row heights, then descend into the blocks. Output branches from each row run toward the left-hand collecting lines. Crossings not drawn as branches do not create additional register-to-register connections.

The main input/output groups continue to the middle drawing, Figure 1b, where the internal-highway interconnection circuit HIC is shown. Figure 1a does not itself draw HIC, the store or the arithmetic unit. No direct line is drawn from AAR to BAR or between the individual stored contents of any pair of registers.

### CPA and CPB paths

CPA and CPB each have one illustrated incoming branch, an empty-circle gate above the register, a downward entry arrow, a downward output through an empty-circle gate below, and a collecting output connection.

These routes are marked 1 & 2 at both input and output. This is distinct from the 1/10 endpoint notation of the wider register/highway paths.

CPA's incoming branch comes from the common input routing at the right. Its output descends to the common outgoing route at the bottom. CPB has the corresponding input and output arrangement on the right. The figure does not show internal selection or decoding logic within either character-position register.

## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

The register-unit description says most RU registers have ten-bit capacity and connect on both input and output to a ten-bit internal parallel data highway. CPA/CPB have two-bit capacity. This gives the width context for 1/10 and 1 & 2; not every bit conductor is drawn.

The prose supplies these functions:

- AAR and BAR hold the two store addresses involved in an instruction.
- SCR holds the next-instruction address for the current routine.
- LIR saves the sequence control number of an interrupted routine during autonomous transfer.
- CBR holds a stack-condition code.
- HDR holds a ten-bit character for input/output transfers.
- MR and NR hold counts altered by circulation around the highway through the interconnection/increment-decrement circuit; both have zero detection.
- CPA and CPB hold character-position state and produce zero/state indications used by CU.

The OCR renders CPB's zero signal inconsistently (including CFB=0); the drawing's BPA=0 and Figure 1c's CPB=0 are therefore recorded separately.

The prose explicitly says all empty circles in Figure 1 represent micro-signal-controlled gates. Individual gate-control wires were omitted for clarity and grouped as CONTROL SIGNALS at CU in Figure 1c.

## Uncertainties and source limits

- BPA=0 beside CPB is not replaced with the expected CPB=0 in the factual inventory.
- Endpoint numbers and braces abbreviate parallel paths. The width statements come from prose; two visible endpoint wires do not mean a two-bit register for AAR–NR.
- No independently named input port is invented for each drawn endpoint.
- The diagram omits segment/modifier facilities and limits the registers to those relevant to the described instructions, according to the prose. It is not a complete inventory of an implemented processor.
- File separation is user-requested; adjoining highway dependencies on Figures 1b/1c remain part of the source context.
