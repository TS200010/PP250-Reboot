The upper area carries device/data-interface paths. The dense lower logic is divided by a dash/dot-style horizontal boundary from that data area. The device interface lies at the left of 4a/4c; the adjoining highway-control interfaces lie at the right of 4b/4d.

The drawing uses:
- Circles with 1 for combining gates.
- Circles with 2, 3, 4 or 5 for multiple-input gating.
- Square triangle/V symbols for inversion.
- Rectangles marked SG1–SG5.
- Divided rectangles marked 1 and 0 for toggles, some horizontal and some vertical.
- Asterisks at selected toggle input terminals.
- Overbars on selected signal/code conditions.

The full logic-function interpretation of these symbols is separately attributed to prose below. A crossing is not automatically a branch: joins are established by continuous bends, dots or explicit T connections. Numerous lines pass through both horizontal and vertical page joins.

### Start, data-register loading and transmit request

On 4a, PROC reaches G1. G1's output enters SG1. SG1's output branches:
- Toward SG2.
- Toward one input of G14.

G14's other input arrives through an inverter from the continuing control route. Its output runs upward into SET BDR.

SG2's output reaches G2 and branches into the G12/reset/control network. G2's output enters TTXD's 1 compartment. TTXD's asserted output runs into G3; G3's output continues across the 4a/4b join as TXSS toward Figure 5. TXSF returns from the right across 4b through an inverter/control route to the transmit-demand network.

G6 combines multiple continuing conditions and outputs HSR leftward. It is shared by initial readiness, destination-result and continuation/termination logic rather than being exclusive to one transaction phase.

DA from 4c joins the common start/acknowledgement network continuing upward into 4a. It is not a second independent pulse-generator pair.

### Destination-result and continue network

Three distinct decoder conditions enter the 4a result gates:
- (J) at G8, whose output sets TFR.
- (L) at G9, whose output sets TFB.
- (B) at G10, whose output enters T3 and the GNE/TDNE network.

The TFR/TFB/TDNE output lines leave as DF/DB/DNE. Their set/reset paths also connect to the common HSR and reset network. The nonexistence path includes stored retry state T3; it is not a direct combinational wire from (B) to DNE.

G13 feeds TCT and CONT. G15 receives (K) and feeds TEOB, which supplies EOB (TX). G16 receives the termination-state route and continues across the join into G17 on 4d. G17 feeds SG5, whose TT output leaves the buffer to the highway-control unit.

TERM enters TTM. Its state and the shared reset network participate in clearing or qualifying the result indications. No independent physical memory of a whole transmitted packet is drawn in these toggles.

## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

### Conventions and logical meaning

The prose calls Figure 4 the buffer unit and Figure 5 the highway drive control unit. The buffer receives device conditions, supplies device indications, and exchanges sequence/condition signals with the selected drive controller.

It explains:
- Circle 1 = OR; larger numeral = AND with that input count.
- A toggle is set by its 1-side input and reset by its 0-side input.
- Starred inputs are trailing-edge activated.
- SG devices are delay/pulse generators.
- Square triangle/V devices invert.
- Parenthesised letters are decoded code conditions.
- Its printed sequence “equations” trace operations and are explicitly **not Boolean statements**.

This capture therefore does not convert degraded OCR equations into a supposedly exact Boolean netlist.

### Protocol meanings supplied by prose

The prose describes BSC states B0, B1 and B2, with B0 idle. It identifies BDR as the data buffer register, PPA as the station's priority address and PHA as its hardware/destination address.

In sending:
1. PROC causes G1/SG1/SG2 activity; SG2 with idle/qualification conditions sets TTXD through G2.
2. TTXD/G3 produces TXSS.
3. The drive controller returns readiness/comparison conditions; BSC advances and HSR is produced.
4. A subsequent PROC loads BDR through G14, removes readiness and produces NIR.
5. Returned strobes and decoded (J)/(L)/(B) conditions produce destination-free, destination-busy or nonexistence/retry handling.
6. Returned valid characters produce CONT/HSR, while (K) produces end-of-block handling.
7. TERM eventually produces TT through G16/G17/SG5 and restores the buffer when the highway-control state clears.

In receiving, H4-related state causes INT and RXSS; a drive-controller return advances BSC. A conditioned SR with (C)/(D)/(E) sets T4, enabling designation, data or queue-empty indications. DA acknowledges the received character, reloads/relaunches it through BDR, clears the indication and produces NIR. End-of-block and queue-full inputs select their codes. Free-highway reception produces EOM, which the device acknowledges with DA.

Response changeover uses CODE REG's S(F)/CR(F) and S(H) paths; it changes sender/receiver roles through the adjoining drive-control unit. The complete role-change state machine is not wholly inside Figure 4.

Queue full produces the (M)/TQF indication on the transmitting side. Queue empty is associated with (E) and the receiving indication network. TX/RX suffixes distinguish the device's role; they are not interchangeable wire names.

### Fault timing and retries

The prose says T7 qualifies the interval between received strobe sense and regenerated ST. G34 detects data mismatch, G37 incompatible returned codes in B1, and G43 code mismatch/incompatibility in B2.

It explains TFT as the immediate fault storage, T8/T9 as retry-related state, and TTF (TX) as the later persistent transmission-fault indication. On the receiver, (G)/G44/TCF and T10/T11 preserve character-fault/repetition state, producing CF or TF (RX). Asterisks matter because several state changes occur at pulse trailing edges.

These meanings come from prose. They do not justify inventing missing clocks, pin polarities or exact equations for an illegible intersection.

## Uncertainties, discrepancies and limits

- The printed DATA (RX) gate reads G26, while the receiving prose names G24 for the data indication. G24 also appears separately on 4d. Both observations are retained; no gate is renamed to make them agree.
- The OCR confuses 0/O, 1/I/l, B/8, G/C, S/5 and toggle labels such as TFT/TF1. This capture uses the inspected drawing labels where legible and does not reproduce OCR substitutions as additional devices.
- Upper comparison glyphs and lower mismatch glyphs are tightly printed. Their logical role is supported by prose; exact typography and some individual gate-pin polarities remain difficult. The capture preserves overbarred J/L/B and K/M qualifications where clear rather than treating them as simultaneously asserted one-hot codes.
- The prose's generic SG count does not consistently match the drawing: SG1–SG5 are visible, including SG5 at TT. No visible generator is omitted to follow the OCR wording.
- Some inverter and rectangular/X element references are absent or not legible. They are not assigned invented names.
- The figure has many unnamed page-edge continuations. The combined capture identifies all discernible named/shared networks, but does not claim a fully pin-resolved, simulation-ready netlist for every ambiguous crossing. Exact reproduction should return to the PDF at the relevant junction.
- Gate control, set/reset and normal/complement outputs must be kept distinct. A wire passing a toggle is not automatically connected to both compartments.
- The source does not provide numerical SG delays, a universal clock, electrical driver levels or complete setup/hold requirements in this drawing.
- Figure 5 and the ring itself remain outside this file. Their named interfaces are retained so the buffer is not falsely described as self-contained.
- These are source-capture limits, not declarations that the repository's wider architectural accounts are unresolved.

INTERPRETIVE NOTE

**Explicit drawing evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** The four joined sections form shared data/code buffering, state control, device handshakes, returned-strobe processing and retry/fault storage. Readiness, receive indications, code selection and fault paths cross the sheet boundaries.

**Explicit patent-text evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** One buffer coordinates a device with a selected ring-highway drive controller, including sender/receiver role changes, returned-character checks and a limited retry procedure.

**Architectural inference:** The state and handshake networks explain why the four sections must be read as one circuit. They do not establish a modern queue implementation, a complete packet memory, or a different arbitration mechanism beyond the adjoining drive controller.
