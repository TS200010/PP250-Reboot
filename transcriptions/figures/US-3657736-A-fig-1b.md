## Direct drawing evidence — PRIMARY EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION

### Components, containment and labels

The centre is labelled SU, STORE UNIT. It contains a large rectangle labelled STORE CS and a smaller horizontal register RSA below it, identified by a leader as STORE ADDRESS REGISTER.

To the left stands SDA, identified as STORE READOUT REGISTER “A”; to the right stands SDB, STORE READOUT REGISTER “B”. Each is a tall narrow rectangle divided into four vertically stacked compartments labelled Q0, Q1, Q2 and Q3, top to bottom.

At the bottom is a rectangle whose multi-line label reads INTERNAL HIGHWAY INTERCONN & ±1 CIRCUIT HIC. The ±1 notation denotes the printed plus/minus-one marking; no internal increment/decrement circuit is expanded.

No overall SU enclosing box is drawn. Circular gate symbols on the wiring are empty and unnumbered.

### Store word readout and register arrangement

STORE CS has an upper pair of endpoint paths marked 1 and 40. These rise into the top distribution wiring, which branches through circular gates to the upper/full-word input routing of SDA and SDB. Arrowheads show the distribution toward those readout-register paths.

The two readout registers are distinct, with separate quadrant subdivisions. Each has gated connections at both ends of every quadrant:

- Eight illustrated endpoint connections on the left side: a pair for each of Q0–Q3.
- Eight illustrated endpoint connections on the right side: another pair for each quadrant.
- The left-side routes collect toward the outgoing internal-highway wiring.
- The right-side routes receive from common input routing, with arrows toward the register.

The pairs represent the endpoint strokes of parallel paths. The drawing does not print a separate bit number beside every quadrant terminal or identify every individual gate.

The top CS distribution and the highway-side register-input routing are separate sources. The quadrant subdivisions and multiple gates must not be collapsed into a single undifferentiated “store output” wire.

### Internal highway and address-register paths

HIC has four illustrated endpoint strokes at each side:

- On the left, an upper 1/10 pair leaves toward RU in Figure 1a, and a lower 1/10 pair enters HIC from RU.
- On the right, an upper 1/10 pair enters HIC from Figure 1c, with leftward arrows into HIC.
- A lower 1/10 pair leaves HIC toward Figure 1c, with rightward arrows.

The connections above HIC link these routed highways with SU:

- Register-output collecting paths from SDA/SDB descend into HIC, with arrows toward the circuit.
- Separate paths rise from HIC into register-input routing through circular gates.
- The input routing branches upward through two illustrated circular gates into RSA.
- RSA's upper paths pass through two more illustrated circular gates and enter the lower edge of STORE CS with upward arrows.

Additional circular gates near the lower SDA/SDB ends control routes joining the store/highway distribution network. They are shown as gate symbols, not separately named functional blocks. The diagram gives no selection truth table or simultaneous-enable rules.

The routes around the top and sides are working data paths with explicit register connections; they are not enclosing borders. Crossings without a branch are not interpreted as additional connections.

### Quadrants and numerical annotations

Q0–Q3 are compartment labels, not separate devices in the figure. The store path uses endpoint markers 1 and 40. The internal-highway paths use 1 and 10. There are no numerical store addresses or register values shown.

The figure prints no separate per-quadrant bit ranges, no capacity in store words and no segment-select circuit. It also does not draw a full store-write path from SDA/SDB back into CS.

Figure 1c's AU/HSE output routing reaches HIC on its right; HIC distributes the return routes back to those units. ## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

The store-unit description explicitly identifies CS, SDA, SDB and RSA. It says a data word has forty bits, divided into four ten-bit quadrants. The register gates permit selection of any quadrant for input or output.

It also says the contents of the readout registers can be written into the addressed store location, but that the corresponding forty-bit gated paths and store input are deliberately omitted from Figure 1b. These missing paths are therefore prose evidence, not wires to add to the factual capture.

The prose identifies HIC as the internal-highway interconnection and increment/decrement circuit. The ten-bit RU/highway context is stated in the introductory register description. The drawn ±1 annotation is consistent with that description but is not a circuit implementation.

The device uses a two-address instruction format, and the prose describes a segmented store. It expressly says segment and modifier facilities are omitted from Figures 1a–1c for presentation. Those facilities are not reconstructed here.

Empty circles are micro-signal-controlled gates; their control wires are grouped at CU in Figure 1c instead of being individually drawn.

## Uncertainties and source limits

- The crowded endpoint routing shows the gated common networks, but not named control signals for each circle. No per-circle enable function is invented.
- Endpoint pairs and the 1/40 and 1/10 labels abbreviate parallel paths; exact widths are explained by the prose rather than by drawing every conductor.
- Store-write ability is explicit in prose, while its path is explicitly omitted from the drawing. It is not silently described as visible.
- No memory capacity, addressing timing or internal form of the ±1 circuit can be read from this overview.
- Separate files preserve the user-requested presentation; they do not separate HIC from its cross-page RU, AU and HSE connections.
