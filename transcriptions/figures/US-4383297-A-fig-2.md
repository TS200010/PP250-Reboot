## Direct drawing evidence — PRIMARY EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION

### Overall layout and grouping

The diagram is labelled `CPU`. With its lettering upright, the B-labelled data/capability blocks occupy the upper left, the A-labelled blocks the upper right, and the ALU the lower left. The bit-manipulation group BM is at the lower right. MAR, MDOR, BCC and ACC lie along the bottom, below the long perimeter-running path. BII enters above the instruction-buffer area at the upper right; BIO leaves below MAR/MDOR.

The outermost solid line has actual connections to block inputs and the ALU result path. It must not be treated simply as an electrically inert CPU enclosure: it forms part of the drawn feedback/distribution wiring. There is no separate enclosing box for a microprogram control unit.

Dashed groupings are:

- A dashed rectangle around the adjacent `BCB` and `BCA/L` sections, labelled **ACF** by a short leader near its upper right.
- A dashed rectangle around `ACA/L` and `ACB`, labelled **BCF** by a leader near its upper left.
- A dashed rectangle labelled `BM`, enclosing `QREG`, `CORR`, `SHIFT1`, `SHIFT2` and `MASK`.

The first two group labels are recorded as printed, despite their apparent reversal relative to the A/B section names and prose. BDF and ADF abut their neighbouring capability sections but are not enclosed as whole blocks by the dashed capability rectangles.

### Input, instruction and A-side selection paths

1. **BII branches to IBL and MDIN.** The incoming path descends into the top of IBL with an arrowhead. A branch runs left across the top of the drawing and turns downward into MDIN, also with an arrowhead.
2. **IBL → IREG.** A downward path from IBL enters the top of IREG. Between the two blocks it branches right to an outward-pointing arrow labelled µPDEC. No destination block for that branch is drawn.
3. **IREG → IMUX.** The path leaves the bottom of IREG, descends and turns left, then enters the right-hand top input of IMUX with a downward arrowhead.
4. **ADF → ADL → IMUX.** ADF has a downward connection to ADL. ADL’s lower path enters the left-hand top input of IMUX with a downward arrowhead.
5. **ACA/L and ACB → right CAPMUX.** Each capability section has a lower connection branching toward the capability multiplexer. The CAPMUX input from ACA/L enters at the left; the ACB path enters at the right. Arrowheads point down into the two inputs.
6. **Right CAPMUX and IMUX converge → SHIFT1.** Their lower outputs join a common line, which descends into SHIFT1 with an arrowhead. This line crosses the separate feedback route to QREG; the crossing is not a junction.
7. **SHIFT1 → SHIFT2.** A downward arrow connects the two shift blocks.
8. **MASK → SHIFT2.** A horizontal line runs left from MASK and enters the right side of SHIFT2 with a leftward arrowhead.
9. **SHIFT2 → ALU A.** The lower SHIFT2 output turns left, extends across the diagram and enters the ALU’s right-hand input A with a downward arrowhead.

These paths preserve both the direct ADF/IREG choice at IMUX and the separate capability-file choice at CAPMUX, followed by their convergence before SHIFT1. No control-input wires for either multiplexer are individually drawn.

### B-side selection and ALU B input

1. **BDF → BDL → BDB.** Downward arrows connect BDF to BDL and BDL to BDB.
2. **BCB and BCA/L → left CAPMUX.** The BCB lower connection feeds the left upper CAPMUX input; the BCA/L path feeds the right upper input. Arrowheads point into CAPMUX.
3. **BDB and left CAPMUX outputs converge.** Their lower paths join a common downward route to the ALU B-side input network.
4. **MDIN joins that B-input network.** The lower MDIN output descends through the centre of the drawing, turns left and connects into the common route feeding B.
5. **HS and COUNT join that network.** Separate lower paths from HS and COUNT turn into the same horizontal B-side route.
6. **QREG/CORR join that network.** QREG’s lower path branches: one branch enters CORR from above with a downward arrowhead; the other runs left/down to the horizontal B-side route. CORR’s lower output also joins that route.
7. **EL and PI connections.** EL’s lower terminal joins a short horizontal route above PI and left of ALU. This route connects to PI’s upper side and to the common vertical stem entering ALU B. PI also has a lower terminal connected to the bottom feedback/distribution line. No arrowhead at the PI upper connection establishes its direction.
8. The shared route ultimately enters the left-hand ALU input labelled B, with a downward arrowhead.

This is a branched common input route, not a series chain passing through HS, COUNT, MDIN, EL and PI in that order. Where an output terminal has no arrowhead, the connection and the arrow at ALU B are the drawing evidence; the capture does not assign an undocumented enable or gating sequence.

### ALU output, feedback and auxiliary inputs

The ALU’s lower output branches to MAR and MDOR and joins the long solid path running around the drawing’s lower, left, upper and right edges.

- One downward branch enters MAR; another enters MDOR.
- Up the left edge, the perimeter-running line reaches the upper BDF/capability row and branches downward into BDF, BCB and BCA/L. BCA/L has two separately drawn upper entry points.
- A branch from the left-side perimeter route runs right above HS and COUNT and has downward arrows into both blocks.
- Along the right and upper edges, the perimeter-running route branches downward into ADF, ACB and ACA/L. ACA/L has two separately drawn upper entry points.
- A separate branch from the right-side route runs left above BM and enters QREG from above with a downward arrowhead.
- PI’s lower terminal joins the bottom route.
- Near the lower right, a branch rises from that route and ends in a rightward arrow labelled ALU CS. No receiving control block is drawn.

The repeated upper file entries and broad perimeter route are visible wiring. They are not a licence to assign specific write, compare or control functions to each file terminal: the drawing provides no terminal names for those entries.

There are two distinct paths into the BM region: the converged CAPMUX/IMUX path into SHIFT1 and the perimeter feedback path into QREG. Their crossing near BM’s top edge is distinct from the explicit CAPMUX/IMUX convergence. Within BM, QREG → CORR and SHIFT1 → SHIFT2 are separate vertical chains; MASK connects only to the depicted side input of SHIFT2. QREG/CORR outputs go to the B-input network, whereas SHIFT2 goes to ALU A.

### BIO and capability-check comparator paths

The lower outputs of MAR and MDOR converge below the blocks. From that common route:

- A downward arrow leaves the drawing at BIO.
- A branch runs right beneath the comparators and turns upward into the bottom terminal of BCC.
- The same horizontal branch continues farther right and turns upward into the bottom terminal of ACC.

Thus BIO is also connected to the two comparators’ lower inputs; the comparator branches are not shown as being in series between MAR/MDOR and the outgoing BIO arrow.

BCC has two upper incoming paths traced from the BCB and BCA/L lower connections. ACC has two upper incoming paths traced from ACB and ACA/L. These paths branch from the capability-section outputs in addition to the CAPMUX paths. Arrowheads point downward into the comparators’ top terminals, while the common BIO route enters from below with upward arrowheads.

The comparator rectangles contain only their names. No separate comparator result output, fault line, comparison operator or connection from a comparator output to ALU is drawn. The bottom arrows are inputs, not comparator outputs.

### Graphic conventions, intersections and annotations

Small open circles appear at many block terminals, including the ALU inputs and output and several register and comparator terminals. They are recorded as terminal marks; no inversion legend or polarity notation establishes that they represent logical NOT operations. Arrowheads supply the directions stated above. Not every connection has an arrowhead at both ends.

The drawing uses small angled bridge-like deviations at numerous crossings of horizontal and vertical lines. In particular, the capability-selection and comparator routes are separate from the long ALU input lines they cross. Explicit branching/convergence has been distinguished from a mere crossing by following each continuous route; wire crossings do not all imply shared nets.

All ordinary blocks are rectangular; ALU uses the notched arithmetic-unit outline. Dashed outlines indicate the three named groupings. There are no separately drawn logic gates, switches or selector-control symbols beyond the named multiplexer blocks. No bus width or clocking/timing convention is given.

The caption is `Fig. 2`, with an underline-like stroke beneath part of the caption. The sheet header reads `U.S. Patent`, `May 10, 1983`, `Sheet 2 of 5`, and `4,383,297`.

## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

The transcription’s brief description identifies Figure 2 as a processing-unit block diagram suitable for the invention. The following explanations are supplied by the prose, not by expanded labels printed in the drawing:

- “DESCRIPTION OF AN ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENT”, paragraph (5), identifies ADF and BDF as A and B data files, each with 32 locations, providing duplicated register arrangements. It identifies ACF and BCF as duplicated capability register files, divided into base sections ACB/BCB and access/limit sections ACA/L and BCA/L. It describes eight general-purpose data registers D(0)–D(7), general-purpose capability registers C(0)–C(7), and special-purpose registers. Those individual registers and sizes are not drawn in Figure 2.
- Paragraph (80) names BM as a bit multiplexer, ALU as the arithmetic unit, IREG as the instruction register, MAR as the memory address register, MDIN as the data-in register, MDOR as the data-out register, and ACC/BCC as A/B capability-check comparators. The later Step S2 calls BM the bit manipulator. Both prose usages refer to BM; neither supplies a detailed expansion of each internal BM label.
- Step S1, paragraph (99), identifies IBL as an instruction buffer. It says incoming instructions pass over BII into IBL, the function code accesses a microprogram unit not shown, and address-offset information goes to IREG. It states that MAR holds a formed address, ACC and BCC check whether access is permitted, and the address passes over BIO to the bus interface BIF shown separately in Figure 3.
- Step S2, paragraph (101), describes IREG’s offset passing through IMUX and BM to ALU A; a capability base from BCB passes through CAPMUX to ALU B. The result is placed in MAR. This describes one selected operation on the paths visible in Figure 2; it does not establish that every depicted block is active on every cycle.
- Step S2, paragraphs (102)–(106), supplies the sequence BII := BIO; MDIN := BII; ALU := MDIN; ALUCS := ALU. It calls ALUCS the arithmetic-unit condition signals and says they convey the generated offset information to the microprogram unit. Figure 2 prints the external label as ALU CS on two lines.
- Steps S3/S4, paragraphs (108)–(113), describe a selected register value passing through ALU to MDOR, then BIO and BII, before reaching MDIN and passing through ALU to the destination register in both ADF and BDF. The BIO-to-BII loopback is explained in the prose via the Figure 3 interface; Figure 2 does not itself draw an external wire joining BIO back to BII.

The indicator-register discussion, paragraph (44), calls the primary indicator register PIR and explicitly associates it with Figures 2 and 4. Figure 2’s short block label reads PI. This is a naming discrepancy preserved below, not a silent alteration of the drawing.

## Discrepancies, uncertainties and limits

- **Capability-group labels:** The dashed group over BCB/BCA/L reads ACF, and that over ACA/L/ACB reads BCF. The prose instead associates the A capability file with ACB/ACA/L and the B file with BCB/BCA/L. The capture preserves both observations. An apparent drawing-label interchange is a possible explanation, not a correction made to the source.
- **PI versus PIR:** The drawing visibly reads PI; the prose says PIR. PI has not been silently transcribed as PIR.
- **Unexpanded abbreviations:** BDL, BDB, ADL, EL, HS, QREG and CORR are retained literally. Their full names and detailed functions are not established by the cited prose passages. COUNT, SHIFT1, SHIFT2 and MASK are printed names; exact counter, shifter and mask behaviour is not specified by their block symbols.
- **µPDEC:** The lettering is captured as µPDEC. The prose’s discussion of microprogram decoding helps contextualise it, but Figure 2 does not draw a decoder block or its detailed control outputs.
- **Terminals and intersections:** The small circles and bridge-like deviations are visible. Their presence does not establish active-low logic, bus width, or a separate hardware element at each intersection.
- **Direction limits:** The capture states arrows where shown and connections where direction is not explicitly marked. Shared routes do not by themselves specify simultaneous driving, bus arbitration, tri-state control, register enables or the microinstruction sequence.
- **Missing control detail:** The prose explicitly says the microprogram unit is not shown. No full control graph, clock scheme or external bus-interface circuit can be recovered from Figure 2 alone.
- **Scope:** These are limits and discrepancies of this source drawing and its prose. They are not declarations that the corresponding architectural questions are unresolved elsewhere in the repository, nor evidence that this later patent’s exact arrangement was present in every earlier System 250 configuration.

INTERPRETIVE NOTE

**Explicit figure evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** The drawing provides two data/capability selection sides feeding a single ALU, with BM on the A-side route, separate capability-check paths, and a distributed ALU-result feedback route. It exposes BII, BIO, µPDEC and ALU CS as labelled paths without drawing the complete external interface or microprogram control.

**Explicit patent-text evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** The files are described as duplicated register arrangements. The load example uses the depicted paths and a bus-interface loopback to access internal register information with ordinary instruction machinery.

**Architectural inference:** These paths are consistent with reuse of the drawn datapath for the patent’s internal-register addressing example. Figure 2 alone does not demonstrate the address-recognition detector, internal-mode masks, control sequence or protection rules. Those require the prose and other figures; they have not been invented or imported into this factual drawing capture.
