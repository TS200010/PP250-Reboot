### Layout and grouping

With the lettering upright, the B-labelled files occupy the upper left and the A-labelled files the upper right. The arithmetic unit is below the B side. A dashed rectangle encloses five blocks on the lower right. MAR, MDOR, BCC and ACC are along the bottom. CPU is printed below the dashed rectangle.

The upper-left file row comprises BDF, BCB and BCA/L, in that order. BCF is printed above the BCB/BCA/L portion. The upper-right row comprises ACA/L, ACB and ADF, in that order; ACF is printed above the ACA/L/ACB portion. There are no dashed capability-file outlines around these two rows.

A long solid route runs around much of the diagram’s perimeter. It has actual branches into block inputs and joins the ALU result route, so it is wiring rather than merely an electrically inert CPU enclosure. A visible interruption occurs in its bottom-right stretch near ALU CS; that scan gap is not silently converted into a separately drawn switch.

### LSB and LSA outputs

## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

The brief description identifies Figure 2 as a typical processor unit suitable for the embodiment. The preferred-embodiment transcription provides these explanations:

- **Paragraph (5):** ADF and BDF are A and B data files, each with 32 locations, providing duplicated register arrangements. ACF and BCF are the A and B capability-register files. These capacities and duplication are prose statements, not numbers printed inside Figure 2.
- **Paragraphs (7)–(12):** The register structure includes data, capability-pointer, capability and indicator registers. P(0)–P(7) hold pointers; corresponding C(0)–C(7) registers obtain base, limit and access information when required. The individual P and C registers are not separately drawn in Figure 2.
- **Paragraphs (50) and (55):** The prose names PIR as the primary indicator register and identifies BM as the bit multiplexer, ALU as the arithmetic unit, IREG as the instruction register, MDIN as data-in, MAR as memory-address, MDOR as data-out, and ACC/BCC as A/B capability-check comparators. The Figure 2 block reads PI rather than PIR, and the dashed-group BM name is prose-supplied.
- **Paragraph (77):** A microprogram control unit controls the operations but is not shown in the drawings. Its outputs and internal sequence must not be invented as extra Figure 2 wiring.
- **Paragraphs (80)–(87):** The instruction arrives in IBL and is gated to IREG. For address formation, the B-side selected capability base passes through CAPMUX to ALU B; IREG’s address value passes through IMUX to ALU A in the stated no-modification case. The ALU adds, MAR receives the result, and a store read is requested.
- **Paragraph (89):** A read pointer enters MDIN over BII from the processor bus.
- **Paragraphs (91)–(95):** Microprogram control selects the pointer registers in both A and B files. The supplied sequence is ALU a := MDIN, AP(x) := ALU, BP(x) := ALU. For a masked load, the prose says a previously loaded mask in D(0) reduces pointer access bits; the transcription renders that register as D(O) in paragraph (95). Figure 2 contains MASK but does not label it D(0) or show that entire operation.
- **Paragraphs (97)–(99):** A load-capability instruction sets the capability’s load-on-use indicator. Its detection occurs when the capability limit code is presented to ACC or BCC; load-set signal LSA or LSB causes the microprogram unit to execute the automatic sequence in Figure 8.
- **Paragraph (104):** Arithmetic-unit condition signals ALU CS may be used to inspect a selected pointer’s access code.

The statement that capability limit information is presented to ACC/BCC and produces a load-set signal explicitly explains the drawn LSA/LSB outputs. It does not mean that the complete load-on-use machinery or Figure 8 flowchart is drawn inside these comparator rectangles.

## Uncertainties and source limits

- **Damaged AD… label:** AD is legible beneath ADF; the remaining lettering is not reliably recoverable from this scan. The position resembles a data-path intermediate block, but a full label such as ADL is not promoted to a drawing fact.
- **Lower bus lettering:** The outgoing label below MAR/MDOR resembles B1O (or B10). BIO is a plausible letter-based normalisation in context, but the scanned middle character is ambiguous. Both forms are retained for searching; no different connection depends on choosing one.
- **Unlabelled instruction branch:** The rightward branch between IBL and IREG has no legible signal name. A name from another patent is not imported.
- **PI/PIR and BM:** PI is the visible block label; PIR and BM occur in the consulted prose. The dashed group’s identification with BM is prose-assisted, not a clearly printed group caption in this scan.
- **Unexpanded labels:** EL, HS, BDL, BDB, QREG and CORR are retained as printed. Full names and detailed operation are not supplied by the cited passages.
- **Scan interruption:** The lower-right perimeter/result-line gap and damage near AD… limit direct continuity evidence there. The capture flags them instead of manufacturing intact strokes.
- **Direction/control limits:** Unarrowed terminals establish connections without specifying their enable or driving rules. The figure does not provide register enables, tri-state behaviour, timing or microinstruction sequencing.
- **Load-on-use scope:** LSA/LSB are drawn and explained by prose, but separate pointer entries, indicator bit positions and the entire deferred-loading algorithm are not drawn here.
- **Historical scope:** This is a source capture of the later US 4,408,274 embodiment. It does not backdate its exact arrangement to every earlier System 250 configuration or declare architectural questions unresolved elsewhere in the repository.

INTERPRETIVE NOTE

**Explicit figure evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** Two data/capability selection sides feed one ALU, with separate comparator inputs, result-distribution wiring, the five-block dashed group, and outgoing LSA/LSB comparator signals.

**Explicit patent-text evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** ACC/BCC detect the load-on-use condition when presented with the capability limit information and issue the load-set signals to the microprogram control unit. The prose describes duplicated pointer-register loading and deferred capability resolution.

**Architectural inference:** The drawing supplies datapath and signal-level support for the prose’s deferred-loading example. It does not by itself specify the detector implementation, pointer/register indexing or the complete loading controller. Those remain prose/other-figure evidence, not inferred circuitry inserted into this capture.
