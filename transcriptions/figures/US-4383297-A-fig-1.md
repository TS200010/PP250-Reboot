## Direct drawing evidence — PRIMARY EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION

### Orientation and arrangement

With the figure lettering upright, four equipment/access-unit assemblies form an upper row. From left to right these are `STA / SAUA`, `STB / SAUB`, `STC / SAUC`, and `PE / PAUN`. Two processor assemblies lie below the connecting lines: `BIF1 / CPU 1` at the left, below STA, and `BIF2 / CPU 2` farther right, roughly below the gap between STB and STC.

Two long, approximately parallel horizontal paths run between the upper row and the processors. The upper horizontal path belongs to `CB1`; the lower belongs to `CB2`. Each reaches all four upper access units. The figure is a single continuous block diagram, with no separately lettered parts.

### CB1 path and branches

1. A vertical stem above BIF1 carries the label CB1. At its bottom an arrowhead points downward into the top edge of BIF1.
2. The same left-hand vertical route extends upward to the left of the two bus entry positions on SAUA, ending with an upward arrowhead into SAUA.
3. Between BIF1 and SAUA this route branches right into the upper horizontal bus path.
4. That horizontal path continues across the diagram. It has upward branches into the left-hand bus entry positions on SAUB and SAUC, each ending with an upward arrowhead at the access-unit boundary.
5. At the right, the path turns upward and ends with an upward arrowhead into the left-hand bus entry position on PAUN.

Thus CB1 is visibly connected to BIF1, SAUA, SAUB, SAUC and PAUN. Its access-unit arrowheads point upward; its BIF1 arrowhead points downward. These are opposing endpoint directions on one branched path, not a single unidirectional arrow from CPU1 to the upper row.

### CB2 path and branches

1. A vertical stem above BIF2 carries the label CB2. At its bottom an arrowhead points downward into the top edge of BIF2.
2. At its upper end this stem joins the lower horizontal bus path.
3. To the left, the lower path runs to the right-hand bus entry position on SAUA, turns upward, crosses the upper horizontal path, and ends with an upward arrowhead into SAUA.
4. A separate upward branch from the lower path reaches the right-hand bus entry position on SAUB. It crosses the upper path and ends with an upward arrowhead into SAUB.
5. Another upward branch reaches the right-hand bus entry position on SAUC, again crossing the upper path and ending with an upward arrowhead into SAUC.
6. To the right, the lower path continues to the right-hand bus entry position on PAUN, turns upward across the upper path’s level, and ends with an upward arrowhead into PAUN.

Thus CB2 is visibly connected to BIF2, SAUA, SAUB, SAUC and PAUN. Its access-unit arrowheads point upward; its BIF2 arrowhead points downward.

### Crossings, grouping and other marks

The CB2 branches to SAUA, SAUB and SAUC cross the upper CB1 horizontal route. The rightmost CB2 branch to PAUN is to the right of the end of that horizontal route. There are no filled junction dots at these crossings. Small angled bends occur where branches meet their own horizontal paths. The two horizontal routes remain separately traceable; a crossing alone is not evidence that CB1 and CB2 are electrically joined.

STA, STB and STC each directly adjoin their corresponding access-unit compartment. No arrows are drawn across those shared boundaries. PE is separated from PAUN by a gap containing five parallel strokes. Those strokes have no arrowheads, labels or individual endpoint names; the drawing does not identify five separate peripheral devices or assign signal meanings to the strokes.

Likewise, no arrow is drawn across either BIF/CPU internal division. There are no visible switches, gates, selectors, register symbols, address comparators, masks, bus widths, timing annotations, legends, explicit feedback loops or control/data subpaths. No direct inter-store, inter-CPU or store-to-PE path is separately drawn outside the two buses and access-unit groupings.

## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

The consulted transcription’s “BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS” describes Figure 1 as a block diagram of a typical data processing system for use with the invention.

The opening paragraphs of “DESCRIPTION OF AN ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENT” explicitly identify:

- CPU1 and CPU2 as processing units.
- STA, STB and STC as storage modules.
- PE as a group of peripheral equipments shown collectively.
- SAUA, SAUB, SAUC and PAUN as access units provided for the storage modules and peripheral equipment.
- CB1 and CB2 as discrete communication paths or buses for CPU1 and CPU2 respectively.
- Each bus as terminating on a separate port of every access unit.
- The access units as able to recognise coded address information presented on the buses terminated at their input ports.

These statements supply the functional names and separate-port explanation; they are not additional symbols visible inside Figure 1.

The summary and the later discussion of the bus interface identify a CPU bus interface unit; this supplies the expansion of BIF. Figure 1 itself prints BIF1 and BIF2 without spelling out their function or showing their internal address-recognition arrangements. The patent’s internal-register addressing, module-number recognition and bit-mask details must not be mistaken for circuitry explicitly drawn in this figure.

## Uncertainties and limits

- All component and bus labels listed above are reasonably legible. No uncertain character has been silently replaced with a different label.
- The drawing provides arrow directions at the endpoints but does not separately label read, write, address, response or control conductors. It does not specify which information travels in each direction or whether transfers can occur simultaneously.
- The interpretation of crossing lines as separate buses is supported by the continuously traceable routes and explicitly confirmed by the prose’s discrete-bus/separate-port description. The drawing does not add bridge symbols at every crossing.
- The five PE–PAUN strokes are visible but unlabelled. Their count is a description of marks, not a documented count of peripheral equipments, registers or signal bits.
- Port numbers, access-unit arbitration, bus electrical organisation, redundancy policy and failure handling are not specified in Figure 1. Their absence here is a limit of this figure capture, not a claim about what the patent or system may describe elsewhere.

INTERPRETIVE NOTE

**Explicit figure evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** Each of two separately labelled CPU-interface paths branches to all three storage access units and the peripheral access unit. Each access unit presents two separately drawn bus entries.

**Explicit patent-text evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** The paths are discrete buses belonging to their respective processors, and each terminates at a separate port on every access unit.

**Architectural inference:** At this diagram’s level, both depicted processors have paths to the same depicted storage and peripheral access units through their respective buses. This is a connectivity inference only. Figure 1 does not establish arbitration behaviour, coherent shared-memory semantics, fault tolerance, or the internal-register access mechanism illustrated elsewhere in the patent.
