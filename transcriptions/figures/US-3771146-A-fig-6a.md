## Direct drawing evidence — PRIMARY EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION

### Grouping and component inventory

The circuit has a dashed rectangle at the left labelled TD by a short leader and a taller dashed rectangle on the right labelled SIR below it. MIS is also printed beside the lower right edge of the SIR outline.

The left TD group contains a two-input circular gate with an overbarred 2. Two labelled inputs enter it: LIM MSB and LIM MSB-1. The gate output leaves to the right and branches upward to TI.

The right SIR group contains:

- MIS11: a narrow vertical rectangle divided horizontally into an upper compartment marked 1 and a lower compartment marked 0.
- MIS14: a second such rectangle below MIS11, also marked 1 above 0.
- A circular gate marked & to the left of MIS14.
- Clock, reset/control and signal connections described below.

A circular gate marked 1 lies outside SIR, below TD and left of the & gate. Its three incoming paths are the MIS11 return branch, 100µs and TU.

No separate G-number is printed beside these three gates. MIS11 and MIS14 are component/indicator labels, not gate identifiers.

### Detector inputs, TI branch and MIS11 path

1. LIM MSB runs right and bends downward into the top of the overbarred-2 gate, with an arrowhead into the gate.
2. LIM MSB-1 runs right and bends upward into the bottom of that gate, also with an arrowhead into the gate.
3. The gate’s output travels horizontally right, crossing the dashed TD boundary and entering MIS11 at the level of its upper, 1-marked compartment.
4. Before SIR, a branch turns upward and ends in an upward arrow labelled TI.
5. MIS11’s output continues right from the upper-compartment level and ends in a rightward arrow labelled TRAP (µCS).
6. A separate branch from that MIS11 output descends, runs left beneath MIS11, then turns downward into the top of the circle marked 1. Its arrowhead points into that gate.

The return to the circle marked 1 is taken from the MIS11 output, after the indicator symbol. It is not a second branch directly from the pre-indicator TI wire.

### Clock and RSS connections

CLK is a vertical line descending inside SIR, to the left of MIS11 and MIS14:

- One branch bends right into the horizontal division/clock-entry region of MIS11, with a rightward arrowhead.
- The vertical line continues downward and bends right into the corresponding region of MIS14, also with a rightward arrowhead.

RSS is a separate horizontal line from the left into MIS11’s lower, 0-marked compartment. It crosses the vertical CLK route without a drawn junction. No RSS branch to MIS14 is drawn.

The CLK line also crosses the horizontal MIS11 return-to-OR route and the lower interrupt-data route. Those crossings are not drawn as branches into CLK. No clock waveform, edge polarity or timing value is supplied beside CLK.

### Trap/periodic/TU convergence and interrupt gating

The circle marked 1 has three visible inputs:

- From above: the return route from MIS11’s output.
- From the left: a rightward arrow on the line labelled 100µs.
- From below: an upward arrow on the line labelled TU.

Its output runs horizontally right, crosses into the SIR outline and enters the left side of the circle marked & with a rightward arrowhead.

µTS enters the bottom of the & gate with an upward arrowhead. The gate’s output travels right into MIS14 at the level of its upper, 1-marked compartment.

MIS14’s output continues to a rightward external arrow labelled INT. No return wire from MIS14 to either gate is shown. No reset input into its 0-marked compartment is drawn.

## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

The SIR description identifies bits 11 and 14 as the trap and interrupt indicators respectively and explicitly associates them with Figure 6a. These are the two divided indicator symbols printed MIS11 and MIS14.

The trap-detector description explicitly calls TD a two-input NAND gate. It identifies the inputs as the two most significant bits of the capability access-type code, labelled LIM MSB and LIM MSB-1, and says TI is zero only when both bits are one. All other combinations give TI one.

The surrounding processor description explains its gate-symbol convention: circles containing 1 are OR gates, while the detector prose specifically resolves the overbarred-2 symbol as NAND. The & symbol visibly supplies the two-input conjunction in the interrupt path. The figure does not require importing a hidden extra inversion stage.

The trap-operation prose assigns these meanings to access-code bit combinations, in its printed order of bit 22 then bit 23:

| Bit 22 | Bit 23 | Prose-supplied meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | Active store-block/segment capability. |
| 1 | 0 | Passive store/backing-block capability. |
| 0 | 1 | Resource capability. |
| 0 | 0 | Null capability. |

That table is patent prose, not a legend printed inside Figure 6a. It supplies the meaning of the detector input combinations. The figure itself does not label which of its two input strokes is bit 22 versus bit 23.

The prose describes trap handling as requiring activity outside the currently running process and a change-process microsequence. Figure 6a itself stops at TI, TRAP (µCS) and INT; it does not draw the change-process machinery, process dump stack or trap-handler entry sequence.

## Uncertainties and limits

- RSS, TU and µTS are legible labels, but their full expansions are not supplied by the cited passages. They are retained literally rather than assigned invented names.
- 100µs is printed beside an incoming line. Reading it as a periodic timing source is consistent with the notation, but no pulse waveform, width or phase relationship to CLK is drawn.
- TRAP is explicitly followed by (µCS). No receiving µPROG block is drawn here, so its full downstream wiring is not added.
- MIS is printed beside the dashed SIR boundary; it is not depicted as a third indicator element.
- The 1/0 symbols support the drawn set/reset-state representation, while CLK supplies the explicitly clocked connections. The drawing does not specify clock edge, setup/hold behaviour or complete latch implementation.
- There is no drawn reset connection for MIS14. Its absence here is not evidence that the implemented interrupt state cannot be cleared.
- The TI combinational output and MIS11’s clocked state/output are distinct places in the drawing. They must not be silently equated in timing or bypassed when describing the path to the OR gate.
- Figure 6b below is a separate FIR detail, not a continuous lower half of Figure 6a. No placeholder or capture for it is created here.
- These are limits of this source figure, not declarations that corresponding architectural mechanisms are unresolved elsewhere in the repository.

INTERPRETIVE NOTE

**Explicit figure evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** TD’s output branches as TI and feeds MIS11. MIS11’s output supplies TRAP (µCS) and one input of the three-input OR path. That OR output is gated by µTS before entering MIS14, whose output is INT. Both indicator symbols have CLK connections.

**Explicit patent-text evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** TD implements NAND recognition of the two high capability access-code bits; SIR bits 11 and 14 are trap and interrupt indicators.

**Architectural inference:** The diagram provides a path from recorded trap state into the interrupt-indicator input alongside the 100µs and TU sources, subject to the µTS gate and indicator clocking. It does not alone establish the scheduling or handler-selection policy that follows INT.
