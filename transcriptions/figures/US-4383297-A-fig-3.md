## Direct drawing evidence — PRIMARY EVIDENCE / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION

### Arrangement, grouping and visible components

The diagram is labelled `BIF`, without a drawn enclosing boundary. Its seven rectangular blocks are arranged as follows, with the labels upright:

| Printed label | Visible position and form |
| --- | --- |
| `BS & C` | Large rectangle in the upper centre/right. |
| `BD&T` | Tall narrow rectangle to the right of BS & C, in the outgoing-bus path. |
| `BR&T` | Tall narrow rectangle at the lower right, in the incoming-bus path. |
| `IDM` | Tall narrow rectangle below BS & C, to the left of BR&T. |
| `PG` | Tall narrow rectangle at the upper left, left of BS & C. |
| `BFI` | Tall narrow rectangle below PG and to the left of IDM. |
| `CLKG` | Small rectangle at the bottom, below and slightly left of IDM. |

There are no separately numbered component callouts, nested component drawings, dashed subgroups, explicit gates, switches or register symbols. BS & C contains only its label; no detector or internal state machine is individually drawn.

External path labels are `BIO`, `BII`, `IM`, `BFIL`, `CLK`, `CBO` and `CBI`. Internal labels are `DB OUT` (searchable compact form: DBOUT), `DB IN` (DBIN), `SI`, and a second occurrence of `IM` beside the BS & C-to-IDM connection. The final character in BIO and CBO is the letter O; BII ends in two letters I.

### BIO entry and its three destinations

The upper-left path labelled BIO runs horizontally right and enters the upper left side of BS & C with a rightward arrowhead. Before reaching BS & C it branches twice:

1. A branch near the left descends and turns right into the upper left side of PG, with an arrowhead pointing into PG.
2. A branch nearer BS & C descends through the central wiring, turns right below BS & C, then descends at the right of IDM and turns left into IDM’s middle right-side terminal. The arrowhead points left into IDM.

Thus BIO visibly reaches BS & C, PG and IDM. The BIO-to-IDM branch is a direct drawn connection, rather than an inferred link through BS & C or the external bus.

On the descent toward IDM, this BIO branch crosses the lower clock-distribution line, the IM stem, and the DBIN line. Those crossings do not merge it with those paths: it continues to its own middle IDM terminal. In particular, the upper IDM right-side terminal is DBIN, and the middle terminal is the separate BIO branch.

### Outgoing external-bus path

Four parallel horizontal lines leave four open-circle terminals on the right side of BS & C and enter the left side of BD&T. Every line has a rightward arrowhead at BD&T.

- The uppermost line is labelled DB OUT.
- The other three parallel lines have no individual names.
- The four drawn lines are not labelled as four bits or four equal-width buses.

From BD&T’s right side, a single horizontal path ends in a rightward arrow labelled CBO. The visible direction is therefore BS & C → BD&T → CBO. No return path from BD&T to BS & C is drawn.

### Incoming external-bus path and received branches

At the lower right, CBI is a horizontal path with a leftward arrowhead entering the right side of BR&T. Two lines leave BR&T’s left side:

1. **DB IN → IDM:** The upper line runs left into the uppermost right-side IDM terminal, with a leftward arrowhead and an open circle at the terminal.
2. **SI → IDM and BFI:** The lower line runs left into the lowest right-side IDM terminal, also with a leftward arrowhead and terminal circle. A branch on this same SI line descends, turns left and enters the middle of BFI’s right side with a leftward arrowhead.

SI therefore has two visibly drawn destinations, IDM and BFI. This includes the IDM connection even though the prose’s short account principally associates status information with BFI.

No individual signals inside CBI, BR&T or SI are drawn or numbered.

### IDM output and BII branch to PG

A horizontal line leaves the left side of IDM and runs to a leftward external arrow labelled BII.

Before reaching the external BII arrow, a branch rises near PG, turns right and enters PG’s lower left side with a rightward arrowhead. Thus the IDM/BII route supplies both the external BII output and a separate PG input.

The rising PG branch crosses the horizontal external IM route without a drawn junction. Similarly, the BII line crosses the separate PG-to-BFI and clock-distribution vertical routes; these crossings do not imply connections.

IDM has three right-side incoming paths in top-to-bottom order: DBIN, BIO loopback, SI. It also has an incoming top path from BS & C and an incoming bottom path from CLKG, as detailed next. The drawing does not provide a truth table assigning any of its inputs to selection states.

### IM path: BS & C to IDM and outward

A line leaves the bottom of BS & C and descends toward the top of IDM. It is labelled IM and has a downward arrowhead entering IDM’s top terminal.

A branch from that stem runs horizontally left to an outward leftward arrow, also labelled IM. Thus the one drawn IM route reaches IDM and also leaves the interface diagram.

The direct BIO loopback route crosses the IM stem on its way to IDM’s middle right-side input. There is no connecting branch at that crossing. IM and the looped-back BIO are separate inputs to IDM.

### Clock-distribution paths

CLKG’s distribution wiring is drawn as follows:

1. A line from its left side runs left and enters the lowest right-side input of BFI with a leftward arrowhead.
2. A branch from that same left-going line rises between PG/BFI and BS & C/IDM. At the top it joins a horizontal route extending both ways: left into PG’s lower right side and right into the lower left side of BS & C. Arrowheads point into both PG and BS & C.
3. A line from CLKG’s upper side rises, turns right, then rises into IDM’s bottom with an upward arrowhead.
4. A separate lower left-going connection from CLKG ends in an external leftward arrow labelled CLK.

Items 1 and 2 are branches of one route; items 3 and 4 are separately drawn connections at CLKG. The visible destination inventory is therefore BFI, PG, BS & C, IDM, and the external CLK arrow.

The drawing assigns no separate signal names, phases, frequencies, polarities or timing relationships to the internal clock branches. The label CLK is printed on the external lower connection.

### PG-to-BFI path and BFI output

PG has the following three arrowed inputs:

- BIO at its upper left side.
- The IDM/BII branch at its lower left side.
- The CLKG-distribution route at its lower right side.

A separate unlabelled line leaves PG’s right side above its lower clock input, runs right, descends, then turns left into the upper right side of BFI. Its arrowhead points into BFI.

This PG-to-BFI line crosses the PG/BS & C clock line, external IM and BII without drawn junctions. Its separate continuity is important: PG’s output is not itself the vertical clock-distribution stem.

BFI has three incoming arrowheads on its right side, from top to bottom:

1. PG’s unlabelled output.
2. SI from BR&T.
3. The CLKG route.

A line leaves BFI’s left side and ends in an external leftward arrow labelled BFIL. No individual fault bits, indicator registers, reset inputs or internal BFI logic are drawn.

### Graphic conventions, labels and numerals

Small open circles appear at the BS & C upper BIO terminal, its four right-side terminals, IDM’s top terminal and its three right-side terminals. They are recorded as drawn terminal marks; no legend establishes an active-low or inversion meaning. Other block connections are drawn without such circles. This capture does not infer polarity from the circles.

Branches and convergences use bends or small angled joins. Ordinary crossed lines often have no bridge symbol or junction dot, so continuity and separately traceable endpoints are necessary to distinguish crossings from connections. No extra components are inferred at intersections.

## Patent prose — PRIMARY EVIDENCE, separately attributed

The brief description identifies Figure 3 as a bus-interface-unit block diagram. Paragraph (82) identifies BIF as the processor’s bus interface and explains that an internal-register module address is recognised there, with the remainder of the address used to access a register. The detector’s circuit is not separately drawn inside BS & C.

Step S1, paragraph (99), supplies the following expansions and operation:

- BIO carries the processor-generated address into the bus interface.
- BS & C is the bus sequence and control circuit.
- BD & T denotes the bus driver and terminator circuits, used to send information to the processor output bus CBO.
- CBI is the processor input bus.
- BR & T denotes bus receiver and terminator circuits.
- DBIN carries the incoming word to IDM, identified as the input data multiplexer.
- SI carries status information to BFI, identified as bus fault indicators.
- Under CLK control, the incoming word passes over BII to the instruction buffer in Figure 2.

The drawing additionally shows SI entering IDM, plus PG and CLKG branches not individually explained by that paragraph. Those drawing connections have been retained rather than omitted to match the prose’s abbreviated account.

Step S2, paragraph (101), says that BS & C detects the internal-mode address; the information is looped back through IDM under the influence of IM. It explicitly describes a detector generating IM when the module-address field is all ones. It also says IM reaches the microprogram unit, which is not shown in the figure.

Paragraphs (102)–(106) give BII := BIO, MDIN := BII, ALU := MDIN, ALUCS := ALU as the subsequent sequence. Only the interface portion is drawn here: the BIO-to-IDM branch, IM connection, and BII output. MDIN, ALU and the microprogram unit are not drawn in Figure 3.

The prose therefore explains how the visible loopback is used; it does not supply a separately drawn all-ones comparator, module-number register, register decoder or mask generator inside this figure.

## Uncertainties and limits

- **Unexpanded abbreviations:** PG and CLKG are legible printed labels. The cited prose passages do not expand them. Their conventional readings as parity generator and clock generator are plausible label-based interpretations, not full names printed in Figure 3 or explicitly supplied by the consulted passages.
- **Three unnamed outgoing lines:** Only the upper BS & C-to-BD&T line is labelled DBOUT. The purposes and widths of the remaining three lines are not established by their marks. Their count must not be converted into a bus-width claim.
- **SI’s extra destination:** The drawing shows SI entering both IDM and BFI. The prose’s assignment of status information to BFI does not explain the separate IDM input. This capture records the connection without inventing its selection behaviour.
- **Unlabelled PG output and clock branches:** Their endpoints and arrows are visible, but their bit widths, encodings and precise functions are not specified.
- **Crossings and terminal circles:** Crossings have been traced by continuity, not treated automatically as junctions. Open terminal circles do not by themselves establish inverted logic.
- **Internal behaviour:** No IDM selection truth table, BS & C state diagram, comparator implementation, clock timing or BFI bit allocation is shown. No assumption is made that an asserted IM suppresses CBO electrically; the drawing alone does not specify that behaviour.
- **Scope of uncertainty:** These are limits of Figure 3 and the consulted prose. They do not declare corresponding architectural questions unresolved elsewhere in the repository or establish this later patent arrangement as universal to earlier System 250 configurations.

INTERPRETIVE NOTE

**Explicit figure evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** BIO has both an outward route through BS & C/BD&T and a separate branch into IDM. IDM also receives external DBIN and SI. IM from BS & C enters IDM and leaves the interface; BII leaves IDM. Distinct PG, fault-indicator and clock paths are also drawn.

**Explicit patent-text evidence / DOCUMENTED OBSERVATION:** BS & C recognises the all-ones internal-mode module address and uses IM to cause the address information to be looped back through IDM, implementing the described BII := BIO transfer.

**Architectural inference:** The diagram and its explanation are consistent with reuse of the processor’s normal outgoing/incoming information paths for the internal-mode example. The direct internal branch provides a route without requiring an externally drawn store response for that example. This does not establish unshown bus suppression, complete register-selection logic, masking circuitry or microprogram sequencing.
